LEO — LOW EARTH ORBIT

Roughly 160–2,000 km up. Fast orbital motion (~90 minute periods), small ground footprints, and low conceptual signal latency. Needs many satellites for continuous global coverage.

MEO — MEDIUM EARTH ORBIT

Roughly 2,000–35,000 km up. Slower motion, much larger footprints per satellite, longer orbital periods (hours). Fewer satellites needed for wide coverage.

GEO — GEOSTATIONARY-LIKE

Around 35,786 km up. Orbital period ≈ 24 hours. An equatorial, prograde orbit at this altitude approximately matches Earth's rotation, so the satellite appears to hover over roughly the same longitude.

WHY ALTITUDE MATTERS

Higher altitude → larger coverage footprint but slower orbital speed and longer period. Lower altitude → small footprint, fast motion, needs more satellites for the same coverage.

WHY INCLINATION MATTERS

Inclination sets the range of latitudes a satellite's ground track can reach. Low inclination stays near the equator; near-90° (polar) orbits sweep over the entire globe as Earth rotates beneath.

ABOUT

Satellite Constellation Sandbox is an educational orbital visualization using simplified orbital mechanics and coverage calculations.
